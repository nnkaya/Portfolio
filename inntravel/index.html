Rebuilding the Booking Journey

Rebuilding the booking journey
around user intent.

Inntravel is a UK-based walking holiday specialist offering highly customisable, high-consideration trips. This project focused on rebuilding the booking journey — improving how users move from initial consideration through to completing a booking or enquiry.

The work evolved across four phases, each responding to what the previous phase revealed.

Discovery + Problems

What we learned from the existing journey.

To understand where and why, the full journey was analysed using funnel data, session recordings, and behavioural patterns, complemented by heuristic review and competitor benchmarks.

Despite strong traffic to the PDP, a significant number of users were dropping off before entering the booking flow — indicating friction early in the journey.

Problems
Finding 01 On the PDP

Key information and pricing were pushed below the fold, with a large hero image dominating the view — Booking and enquiry were also combined behind a single action.

Finding 02 Checkout

A dense 5-step checkout, with the highest drop-off at Step 1, where key selections weren’t visible and users had to recall information from the PDP.

Finding 03 Enquiry path

Users who preferred to enquire had to leave the page entirely and fill out a long generic form.

The Opportunity

Better support progression through the journey by aligning the experience with user intent and reducing friction at key steps.

How the work evolved

Early changes focused on improving the existing experience, while later phases responded to new constraints and a shift towards e-commerce.

Working within constraints

Designing within operational and e-commerce constraints.

Inntravel’s holidays are highly customisable — a key part of their value proposition — making it difficult to apply standard e-commerce patterns without oversimplifying the experience.

The Lemax system defined what data could be shown and how the booking flow behaved — including limitations around availability, pricing, and response speed.

The audience ranges from users who need guidance to those expecting fast, streamlined digital experiences.

Drop-off was highest at the PDP — making it the starting point.

Phase 1

Restructuring the PDP to create a clear path forward.

Focused on improving clarity at the point of entry — making key information visible early and helping users understand both the content and the next step.

  • Highlights above the fold — key information surfaced earlier to support quick evaluation
  • Separated Book and Enquire CTAs — clearer paths based on user intent
  • Pricing surfaced earlier — no longer hidden below the fold
  • Gallery over hero image — to give users a better sense of the full experience

Phase 2

Improving the existing checkout experience.

With more users entering the flow, checkout became the main bottleneck. Data showed drop-offs at Step 1 and payment steps.

  • Grouped related inputs — reducing cognitive load
  • Replaced recall-heavy inputs — using in-flow selections (dropdowns, pre-filled options)
  • Combined review and payment — into a single step
  • Introduced a call-back option — as an alternative path

Phase 3

From a legacy form to a modern booking experience.

This phase focused less on immediate uplift, and more on creating a booking structure flexible enough to support future e-commerce growth.

I worked within the constraints of the Lemax system, shaping the experience around what data could be surfaced.

View live product

Despite restructuring the flow, conversion remained largely unchanged — suggesting that the issue wasn't just how the flow worked, but whether it matched user intent.

Phase 4

Supporting different levels of user intent.

Not all users were ready to book immediately.

Introducing a lightweight enquiry option on the PDP allowed users to act without committing — capturing demand that was previously lost.

This contributed to an increase in total leads (bookings + enquiries), expanding conversion beyond the booking flow.

Closing

Designing for booking intent meant supporting more than one path forward.

Each phase addressed a specific part of the journey, and each one revealed something the next phase needed to solve.

Reflection Looking back across phases

The project highlighted how important it is to support different decision-making behaviours within the same journey.
Not all users arrive ready to book — and designing for both commitment and consideration can significantly improve overall engagement.
